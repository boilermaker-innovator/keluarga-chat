QR Pay: Indonesia vs Australia

The same plate of nasi goreng, paid for two ways. Indonesia already has one QR code that every banking and e-wallet app can scan. Australia doesn't yet. Here's what each one looks like for a small food stall.

🇮🇩 Indonesia · QRIS

Live today
DEMO · NOT A REAL MERCHANT
Sample code for Rp 85.000. Scanning it won't pay anyone.

When a customer scans

  1. They open whatever app they already use: a bank app (BCA mobile, Livin' by Mandiri, BRImo) or an e-wallet (GoPay, OVO, DANA, ShopeePay).
  2. The app reads the code and shows "WARUNG DEMO · Rp 85.000". If the code has no price in it, they type the amount.
  3. They confirm with a PIN or fingerprint. The stall hears a "pembayaran berhasil" (payment received) chime or sees it on their phone.
Which app
Any one. Every QRIS code works with every QRIS app, so the stall needs only one code, not a sticker for each wallet.
Where the money goes
Through the national QRIS switch to the stall's own bank or e-wallet account, usually the same day or the next business day.
What it costs the stall
Rp 0 here. For micro businesses, Bank Indonesia sets a 0% fee on sales up to Rp 100.000 and 0.3% above that. Bigger businesses pay around 0.7%. The customer pays nothing.

🇦🇺 Australia · PayID QR

How it could work
DEMO · NOT A REAL MERCHANT
Concept code for A$8.00. No bank app will accept it.

When a customer scans

  1. They open their own bank app (CommBank, NAB, Westpac, ANZ, ING, Up…) and choose pay or scan. Today, many bank apps can't scan a payment QR yet, so they'd type the PayID instead.
  2. The app looks up the PayID and shows the registered name, "WARUNG DEMO", so they can check they're paying the right business. The A$8.00 and reference fill in from the code.
  3. They confirm. Through the New Payments Platform (Osko), the money usually arrives in under a minute, any time, any day.
Which app
The customer's normal banking app. No extra wallet to download, but there's no single scan standard that every Australian bank app supports yet.
Where the money goes
Straight into the stall's bank account linked to their PayID (an email, mobile number or ABN). No card scheme sits in the middle.
What it costs the stall
Usually $0. Most banks don't charge to receive a PayID payment, though some business accounts have a small per-transaction fee. A card tap for the same $8 typically costs the stall 1–2%, plus terminal rental.

How a QRIS code is built

A QRIS code is just a line of text in a QR square. It follows EMV QR, the international merchant QR standard written by EMVCo (the group behind chip cards). The text is a chain of small blocks, and every block has the same three parts:

58ID: what it is 02Length: 2 characters IDValue: Indonesia

Tag 58 is always the country, so 5802ID means "country: ID". Some blocks hold smaller blocks inside them, built the same way. These four matter most:

26 / 51 Merchant ID
Who gets paid. Tag 51 holds the stall's national merchant ID (NMID); tag 26 says which bank or e-wallet signed the stall up.
54 Amount
Optional. If it's there, the app fills in the price. If not, the customer types it. One sticker on the cart has no amount; a code shown on a till for one order does.
58 Country code
"ID" for Indonesia. Tag 53 next to it gives the currency: 360 means rupiah.
63 Checksum
Always the last block: a 4-character CRC worked out from everything before it. Change one character and it no longer matches, so the app refuses the code.
